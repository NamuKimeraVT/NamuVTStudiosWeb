Where was NamuVT Studios founded?

NamuVT Studios was founded in Buenos Aires, Argentina.

What are the 25 applications in the NVTS ecosystem?

The official catalog consists of NVTS-PhotoMania, NVTS-MixerDAW, NVTS-PremiereSurprise, NVTS-MultiEncoder, NVTS-FusionFX, NVTS-Vectorize, NVTS-Docs, NVTS-Sheets, NVTS-Deck, NVTS-Pixelize, NVTS-Designer, NVTS-Oven, NVTS-FigGUI, NVTS-GameEngine, NVTS-SimulationEngine, NVTS-Symphony, NVTS-CAD, NVTS-Works, NVTS-PDF, NVTS-Light, NVTS-Codex, NVTS-Civil, NVTS-BIM, NVTS-ProtoType, and NVTS-Matrix. NVTS-Launcher is the complementary desktop application. Procedural Field Maker remains an independent, available tool.

The ecosystem covers photo editing and management, illustration, audio, video, media encoding, visual effects, vector and editorial design, documents, productivity, pixel art, 3D creation, interface design, software development, video games, 2D animation, simulation for research and games, CAD, civil engineering, BIM, technical prototyping, industrial design, and studio management.

  • Flameshot: capturing and annotating screenshots for documentation and issue reporting.
  • GB Studio: creating games inspired by the style and constraints of classic handheld platforms.
  • GitHub Desktop: visual management of repositories, branches, commits, and synchronized changes.
  • Jira: planning and tracking tasks, issues, and organized work through agile workflows.
  • Krita: digital illustration, painting, concept art, and 2D visual assets.
  • Meld Studio: recording and producing audiovisual content and gameplay sessions.
  • NVTS-Works: internal management of projects, teams, tasks, issues, priorities, calendars, and work progress.
  • PhotoCraft: image editing and compositing, retouching, and preparation of visual assets.

Which libraries are part of the NVTS ecosystem?

The following libraries and technologies are included in the ecosystem's planned technical scope. They support rendering, imaging, physics, geometry, media, and other shared capabilities.

  • Assimp: importing and processing 3D model assets.
  • Babylon.js: real-time 3D rendering, scenes, materials, lighting, and browser-based interaction.
  • Box2D: 2D rigid-body physics, collision detection, and simulation.
  • Bullet: 3D physics simulation, collision detection, and rigid-body dynamics.
  • Cannon: 3D rigid-body physics and collision simulation.
  • CanvasKit: high-performance 2D graphics and canvas rendering.
  • FFmpeg: audio and video decoding, encoding, conversion, and processing.
  • Filament: physically based real-time rendering.
  • GSAP: animation timelines and interface motion.
  • libigl: geometry processing using selected non-copyleft modules only; copyleft headers are excluded.
  • libvips: high-performance image processing and conversion.
  • OCCT (Open CASCADE Technology): CAD geometry, boundary representation, and modeling operations. Its LGPL terms and exception are handled with the required notices.
  • OpenColorIO: color management and consistent color workflows.
  • OpenCV (including contrib modules): computer vision and image-processing functions; individual modules are reviewed before inclusion.
  • OpenImageIO: reading, writing, and processing professional image formats.
  • OpenVDB: sparse volumetric data structures and volume workflows.
  • PixiJS: accelerated 2D graphics rendering and browser-based visual interfaces.
  • Rapier 2D: 2D rigid-body physics and collision detection.
  • Rapier 3D: 3D rigid-body physics and collision detection.
  • SoLoud: cross-platform audio playback and sound management.
  • wgpu: modern cross-platform GPU access and graphics compute.

Licensing is reviewed at the component and module level. Dependencies retain their respective license notices, and components incompatible with the ecosystem's MIT distribution model are excluded. CGAL is not included because the reviewed packages of interest are GPL-3.0-or-later. For libigl, the copyleft directory is excluded; only appropriately licensed modules may be used.
